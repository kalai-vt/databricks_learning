# Risk & Compliance — Fraud Response Policy (Confidential)

## Initial triage
When a customer reports a suspected fraudulent transaction, front-line staff and any AI
assistant supporting them must treat it as a human-in-the-loop case: the assistant may
explain the general dispute process and required documentation, but must not confirm or
deny whether fraud occurred, and must not promise a specific reimbursement outcome or
timeline beyond the standard regulatory window.

## Regulation E timelines
For unauthorized electronic fund transfers, the bank must provide provisional credit
within 10 business days of the report (extendable to 20 days for new accounts) while the
investigation continues, and must complete the investigation within 45 days (90 days for
certain transaction types).

## Case escalation matrix
Reported amounts under $1,000 with a clear unauthorized-transaction pattern may be
handled by tier-1 fraud operations. Amounts over $1,000, cases involving suspected
account takeover, or cases with a suspected insider component must be escalated to the
Fraud Investigations unit and Risk & Compliance within 24 hours.

## Communication constraints
Staff and AI assistants must never ask a customer to share their full card number,
CVV, online banking password, or one-time passcode over chat or phone in the course of
investigating a fraud report — this mirrors the exact technique fraudsters use, and
doing so is a policy violation regardless of intent.

## Suspicious activity reporting cross-reference
Confirmed fraud cases above the regulatory threshold must also be evaluated for SAR
filing per the KYC/AML policy, coordinated jointly with the AML team.
