# Retail Banking — KYC / AML Policy Summary

## Customer identification program (CIP)
Before opening any account, staff must collect and verify the customer's full legal
name, date of birth, residential address, and identification number (SSN or equivalent
national ID). Acceptable ID documents include an unexpired government-issued photo ID
(driver's license, passport, or state ID card).

## Enhanced due diligence (EDD) triggers
Enhanced due diligence is required for: politically exposed persons (PEPs), customers
from jurisdictions on the internal high-risk country list, cash-intensive businesses,
and any account with expected monthly activity above $50,000. EDD includes source-of-
funds documentation and a supervisor sign-off before the account is activated.

## Suspicious activity reporting (SAR)
Any transaction pattern that appears designed to evade reporting thresholds (structuring),
lacks an apparent lawful purpose, or matches a typology in the AML training materials
must be escalated to the Risk & Compliance team within one business day. Staff must not
disclose to the customer that a SAR is being filed or considered ("tipping off" is
prohibited by law).

## Recordkeeping
Customer identification records must be retained for five years after account closure.
Transaction records supporting a SAR filing are retained for five years from the filing
date, per BSA/AML recordkeeping requirements.

## Training requirement
All customer-facing staff must complete annual AML/KYC refresher training. New hires
must complete initial training before they are authorized to open accounts
independently.
