# Retail Banking — Loan Underwriting Policy (Confidential)

## Scope
This policy governs underwriting decisions for consumer personal loans, auto loans, and
unsecured lines of credit originated through retail channels. Access is restricted to
risk analysts, compliance officers, and administrators — front-line tellers do not have
underwriting authority and must refer applicants to a licensed loan officer.

## Permitted underwriting factors
Underwriting decisions may consider: verified income, existing debt obligations
(debt-to-income ratio), credit history and score, employment stability, and loan
purpose. Decisions must NOT consider race, color, religion, national origin, sex,
marital status, age (provided the applicant has capacity to contract), receipt of public
assistance income, or the applicant's exercise of rights under the Consumer Credit
Protection Act. This is a hard compliance boundary under the Equal Credit Opportunity
Act (ECOA) and Regulation B, and any model or assistant used in the process must be able
to demonstrate none of these factors influenced the outcome.

## Adverse action requirements
When an application is declined, the system must generate a Regulation B-compliant
adverse action notice within 30 days, citing the specific principal reason(s) for
denial (e.g., "debt-to-income ratio exceeds product maximum"), not a generic statement.

## Model governance for automated scoring
Any statistical or AI-assisted scoring model used in underwriting must be validated
annually for disparate impact across protected classes, documented in a model card, and
reviewed by the Model Risk Management committee before deployment or after any material
retraining.

## Escalation
Any underwriting-adjacent AI assistant output must be treated as informational only.
Final approve/decline decisions require sign-off from an underwriter with delegated
lending authority; the assistant must never issue a binding decision.
